# ECI Job Orchestrator

This module defines Job-level coordination within one ECI lifecycle. It changes logical ownership only; it does not change Worker runtime authority, hooks, or planner authority.

## Counts and ownership

- **N** counts admitted outcomes until acceptance or completed cancellation closure, including queued, waiting, blocked, post-Fast, and cancellation-cleanup outcomes. Reopening an accepted outcome counts it again. N governs lifecycle and acceptance only; it never publishes or retires Orchestrators.
- Give each independently requested root work package one stable **Job** identity. A Job may recursively spawn child Jobs under the admission rule below. Record one immutable parent Job for each child; root Jobs have no parent. Ordinary outcome decomposition, extensions, repairs, iterations, main/Fast paths, Helpers, owner transfers, and shared targets retain their outcome’s Job unless admitted as a distinct child package. Each outcome has one home Job; reopening restores it and its ancestry.
- **J** counts every unclosed Job once across the whole lifecycle. A Job closes only when all home outcomes are accepted or cancellation-closed, all child Jobs are closed, all subtree Writers are observed stopped, and all produced reports are accounted for. Pending transfers, report drains, and cancellation cleanup keep the Job counted. A parent remains counted while descendants continue, even after its home outcomes close.
- Each active Job has exactly one published Orchestrator, including a single Job. That Orchestrator coordinates all outcomes in its Job directly through their Workers; do not add a separate outcome owner beneath it. Publication rules: [Publication, transfer, and report drain](#publication-transfer-and-report-drain).
- A new outcome within an active Job changes that Job owner’s outcome set, not the number of Orchestrators. N transitions alone never create or retire an Orchestrator.

| Active work | Logical ownership |
| --- | --- |
| Job A contains A1/A2; J = 1, N = 2 | Orchestrator A coordinates both outcomes. |
| Job A contains A1/A2; Job B contains B1; J = 2, N = 3 | Exactly two Orchestrators: A coordinates A1/A2; B coordinates B1. |
| Job B satisfies the closure predicate, leaving A1/A2; J = 1 while N = 2 | Orchestrator A continues to coordinate A1/A2. |
| A → B → C; N = 0; C has pending Writer/report cleanup; J = 3 | A/B/C remain counted until the subtree satisfies closure; then J = 0. |

At N = 0, the Supervisor performs ordinary lifecycle teardown only after every Job satisfies the closure predicate above.

## Recursive child admission and closure

- The current Job owner may admit a bounded child package within inherited user authorization: decomposition, delegated deliverables, or related work covered by the task mandate. Producers and Helpers propose a child to their Job owner; spawning a Helper alone creates no Job. Record the parent, requested outcome, authorization source, scope, dependencies, acceptance criteria, and reporting route. Children may admit further children by the same rule. Do not ask the user again merely because authorized work receives a child Job.
- Admit new work only within that authority. Relevance, a discovery, severity, or a deadline alone does not authorize unrelated work. Keep unadmitted separate-outcome observations as follow-ups; seek user direction only for an actual authorization gap. A bounded, authorized nonblocking repair may be a child; it does not become a parent acceptance criterion merely through admission.
- Report admission and the effective owner route upward through parent owners to the root Supervisor. Apply the publisher protocol below within root capacity decisions. The root Supervisor maintains global N/J, ownership visibility, and index matching across all depths. Parent edges survive transfers and J changes; ownership transfer never flattens the Job tree. Protected control, global lookup, commit verification, adjudication, and acceptance remain with the root Supervisor.
- Each child has its own requirements and full ECI pipeline: concurrent main and Fast Producers, fresh independent critics, post-Fast restart, required verification, and separate acceptance evidence. Parent exploration, Fast success, reviews, or checkpoint never substitute for the child's pipeline. Dispatch ready independent parent, child, and sibling work concurrently; serialize only actual dependencies, overlapping mutable state, or capacity.
- Child owners report progress, findings, checked ranges, review evidence, and closure recommendations upward to their parent owner, which relays material state to the Supervisor. Supervisor-owned decisions and global index lookups from a child Job take the same parent route. The Supervisor sends its decisions, lookup results, and other follow-ups for a child Job, including those for its Producers and Helpers, through the parent owner, not directly; protected controls such as `write proc://<id>/kill` stay direct. Parent ownership accounts for descendants but does not confer Producer write authority or independent-review authority.
- Accept each outcome independently under Supervisor authority. A parent's home outcomes may finish while descendants continue; apply the canonical Job closure predicate above. Cancel a Job recursively through its descendants, preserve changes and evidence, and leave unrelated siblings running. Child completion or cancellation never tears down the root lifecycle. Only the root Supervisor performs root teardown after the whole tree closes.

## Authority and reporting

- The Supervisor owns root outcome admission, global admission visibility and closure, N/J tracking, global capacity, protected controls, cross-Job visibility, final impact/treatment/dispositions, independent verification of Producer commits, acceptance, and lifecycle teardown. Current Job owners admit authorized child packages under the recursive admission rule.
- An Orchestrator owns routine dispatch, continuation, sequencing, dependencies, evidence reconciliation, progress, child admission and child-owner publication, and closure preparation for its published Job. It coordinates every home outcome directly and its child Jobs through their owners, and loads the task-local review policy for review dispatch. It cannot enlarge authorization, publish an unrelated owner, accept work, or close the root lifecycle.
- Preserve each outcome’s requirements, bounded assignments, Producer identities, main/Fast paths, independent reviews, post-Fast sequence, required E2E, and acceptance evidence. One Job owner never combines outcomes or their acceptance.
- Name each assignment’s Job, parent chain, outcome, Supervisor runtime identity, and current published Job-owner runtime identity. Use [canonical assignment preparation](../../context-ledger/requirement-assignment.md) with that context in operational instructions and forward its returned payload unchanged through native tools. Workers send task-local reports directly to the current owner: `write agent://<id>` to an Orchestrator. Child owners report through parent owners; send Supervisor-owned decisions to the Supervisor (for a child Job, through its parent owner). Automatic runtime-parent delivery does not replace explicit reporting.
- A Producer sends its committed handoff through the Job owner to the Supervisor. The Supervisor verifies the exact immutable commit range before dependent review or work. It returns the verified range and outcome mapping to every affected Job owner (for a child Job, through its parent owner). Continue outcome-local work whose dependencies are already satisfied.
- Job coordination does not grant authority to assign disputed content or schedule Git turns. The Supervisor and Orchestrators report identity, scope, and index facts. Responsible Producers decide implementation boundaries and commit order directly; Helpers report through their parents and follow their responsible Producer’s agreement.
- **Messages to a Job:** the Supervisor and Orchestrators apply the [role rule](../SKILL.md#activation-and-invariants) ("design no solution") to everything they send to an owner or Worker they coordinate, including every `task` field, brief, relay, lookup reply, follow-up, and correction, and to their dispatch reports.
  - Quote each relevant user message with its source for the `exact user source → faithful requested outcome → bounded scope` chain. Quote or cite, with its author, only the part of an earlier passage the user's words refer to. Content the user chooses from it, such as "do option 2", is user-set; approving an analysis, such as "good tensions", sets nothing beyond the user's words.
  - Give evidence as observed facts with sources. Tag your readings of the user's words and the evidence `[INFERENCE]`.
  - Keep the required operational instructions, including the SSOT checkout and branch and shared-index coordination. Scope gives authorization bounds and excludes only what the user excluded, outcomes the user did not request, other owners' scope, and unrelated dirty work. Acceptance restates the requested outcome. Targets and further acceptance criteria come only from the user's words, a Step 2 selection, or a review finding with the Supervisor's treatment. Route skills from the request, user-set constraints, evidence, and any Step 2 selection; the Job owner routes design-dependent skills. Policy files the recipient loads apply without restating.
  - Report a dispatch as an assignment under [Work estimates](../../../eci-binding-contract.md#work-estimates), with the requested outcome as its expected result. Report designs and changes only as the Job produces them.

## Publication, transfer, and report drain

- The publisher is the root Supervisor for root Jobs and the current parent Job owner for child Jobs. For initial publication, the publisher is the named interim owner; during transfer, the outgoing owner remains effective until publication. Child publication follows root capacity decisions; a changed parent owner takes over pending child transfers and report holds.
- **Initial admission:** before the first Worker spawn for any Job, root or child, the Job is declared in the ledger as `work:<job>:job` (`owner`, `state: active`, and an `outcomes` link to each admitted outcome record) and its Orchestrator is published. This ordering is the one exception to [ledger update priority](../../context-ledger/SKILL.md#update-points); it is a spawn order, not a permission check. Do not spawn a Worker for a Job until its Orchestrator is published. For multiple Jobs admitted together, prepare one Orchestrator candidate per Job (the prepared, unpublished owner) from the complete admitted set; publish one owner per Job, not one per outcome. If capacity is short, the publisher queues the candidate and publishes it when capacity frees: the Supervisor for a root Job, the parent Job owner for a child Job.
- **Job publication:** each publisher prepares one Orchestrator for each active Job it publishes without one, under the spawn rule in the Initial admission bullet above. Each transfer covers every home outcome.
- **Job admission:** prepare and publish its Orchestrator. Adding or closing outcomes in existing Jobs changes their owners’ assignments, not the number of Orchestrators.
- **Before publication:** the outgoing owner stops new dispatch only for the transferring Job, finishes or accounts for its active coordination call, and transfers requirements, scopes, assignments, evidence, outstanding reports, and current state for every home outcome, plus child-owner routes and subtree accountability. Hold affected Job-local reports during transfer; route Supervisor-owned decisions to the Supervisor (for a child Job, through its parent owner). Existing Workers continue bounded work. If the outgoing owner is unreachable (its `write agent://<id>` failed), the publisher performs this transfer from the outgoing owner's `history://<id>` transcript and the ledger.
- **Publication:** the incoming candidate accepts the bounded ownership transfer. The publisher rechecks Job membership, scope, current ownership, and the current parent owner before publishing the single effective owner. Notify incoming and outgoing owners and existing Workers; immediate child owners receive the new upward reporting route. Transfer held reports through the publisher. If scope or the parent owner changed, withdraw or recompute; preparation alone grants no dispatch authority.
- **Retirement and delayed reports:** an owner retires from dispatch when its entire Job subtree closes. Closing its last home outcome does not retire an owner with unfinished descendants. Stay published for relay until already-produced reports are accounted for. Former, withdrawn, and non-current owners relay reports through the current parent route to the Supervisor without dispatch. The Supervisor decides whether closed-outcome evidence reopens work and forwards an unprocessed active-outcome report to its current owner (for a child Job, through its parent owner). Do not duplicate already-routed reports or issued reviews.
- **Migration:** transfer any existing per-outcome owner through this protocol. Consolidate all home outcomes of each Job under one published Job Orchestrator. Retire superseded owners to report relay only. Preserve child Job edges and their independent pipelines; remove duplicate outcome owners, not Job ancestry.
- Ownership transfer preserves Producer and reviewer identities, assignments, and runtime ancestry. It does not restart Workers, wait for their acknowledgements, or stop Writers solely because ownership changed. Root teardown uses the canonical closure predicate.

Workers explicitly deliver progress and final reports before returning. Preserve the Job, outcome, assignment, original source, report, and evidence references. Use `write agent://<id>` for an Orchestrator and for the Supervisor. A published owner processes reports within its assignment except during a transfer hold. A non-current owner relays through the current logical parent route without dispatch, implementation, review, disposition, acceptance, or lifecycle action; root-Job reports go to the Supervisor. Forward only reports that still need current-owner processing; already-routed evidence needs no duplicate followup. A timeout alone does not trigger a drain.

## Job hold and tool errors

- The published Orchestrator holds its Job until the Job closes under the closure predicate or the Supervisor tears it down. A report, an outcome ready for acceptance, or a stand-by does not release the Job. See [Counts and ownership](#counts-and-ownership).
- When nothing is actionable, it parks under the Parking rule in `~/.omp/agent/RULES.md`. On a tool error, it reads `proc://` to check its Workers and continues any actionable work, else parks. A tool error never releases the Job.
- A cancel of this owner's own run, by the Supervisor or parent owner with `write proc://<id>/kill`, or by a user or a runtime stop, is not a tool error: it ends the turn, and `Operation aborted` from a tool is the cancel signature. If the turn continues after that result, treat it as a tool error under the bullet above. The Job stays open and this owner stays published. On revival after a cancel, it reads `proc://` for Workers that may still run, then acts, parks again, or reports the remaining state. Cancelling one of its Workers returns a normal result and does not end the turn. See [Counts and ownership](#counts-and-ownership).
- Before parking, it sends its parent owner (for a root Job, the Supervisor) only Job state not yet reported, by `write agent://<parent owner>`. It stays idle as the still-published owner until revived. See [Authority and reporting](#authority-and-reporting).

## Active Job compliance

The current Job owner immediately critiques significant-movement and final Worker reports against actual work, original requirements, and applicable ECI duties. Check role authority, applicable ECI dependency skills and their methods, model selection, delegation, and completion evidence; a positive report or unchanged assignment is insufficient. Resolve concrete deviations through the responsible Worker and verify its correction. Continue unaffected work.

Retain the last roster-review time in existing coordination state. About every half-hour while the Job actively executes, refresh its active roster at the next normal coordination cycle (a revival counts): actor, runtime parent, assigned role/profile, and actual current work, including Helpers. The current owner accounts for descendants. Explicitly confirm or critique each actor's ECI compliance from current assignments, reports, and relevant evidence; retain the verdict, correction, or unknown and next check. Reuse current reports; request a focused activity update when stale or unclear. Send the roster and findings to the Supervisor in the ordinary Job update. Update changed entries when significant movement is processed and carry the last check through ownership transfer. Do one current review when due; add no catch-up bursts, idle wakeups, background polling, separate registry format, or checker agents.

## Active-writer rows and shared-index coordination

These rules apply to every actor authorized to stage or otherwise mutate the Git index. Only Producers commit. Assign every new tracked repository contribution—including documentation, configuration, analysis, and administrative deliverables—to a Producer to author, check, and commit. Supervisors and Orchestrators author no new tracked contribution, stage no repository change, and commit nothing. Preserve any pre-existing authorized Supervisor-authored content only through an explicit bounded handoff that records its original author, scope, and provenance. Participation adds no edit authority; Helpers follow their responsible Producer’s agreement and never commit.

The [typed Worker Git route](../../../eci/hooks/gate-escape-hatches.md) is unavailable during active ECI: `$HOME/.omp/agent/eci/bin/eci-worker-git --repo <repository>` is built but not launcher-wired. Workers use native Git, preserving the original repository context and applicable effect checks. Unstage exact owned paths with native Git, preserving unrelated staged content, and correct committed content in a new prepared-index checkpoint. Amendments, history rewrites, and arbitrary Git argument passthrough remain unsupported by typed operations; the catalog's `run-once` exception (explicit user authorization for the exact command and environment, a nonblank reason, and acknowledgment) is unavailable while `eci-worker-git` is not launcher-wired. The CLI cannot verify Producer assignment, hunk ownership, or completed coordination; the rules below still apply even under that exact authorized exception.

1. Each Producer reports only its own active-writer row to its current published Job owner. Other authorized index actors, including Helpers, report through their current owner or responsible Producer. Each row identifies the exact runtime identity, contribution scope, responsible Producer, home Job and outcome where applicable, repository/worktree, declared write paths or regions, actual Git index identity, current branch, live pending-intent state, and report-owner identity. A Producer row names any pre-existing Supervisor-authored scope explicitly handed to it. For every write-capable Helper descendant, pair its exact runtime identity with its write scope in the responsible Producer’s row. Preserve those pairings and the responsible Producer’s identity in returned matching peer data. An authorized Helper index operation names the actual actor. Use existing assignments and reports, not a separate registry.
2. Rows match when declared write scopes may intersect or actors have live pending intents on the same actual Git index. The published Job owner matches rows across all its home outcomes and reports them through ordinary coordination to the Supervisor. The Supervisor matches across every Job and descendant depth and returns only matching peer rows through published Job owners; a child Job's owner receives them through its parent owner. Same-index matching ignores path, Job, ancestry, outcome, and branch differences, including different outcomes in the same Job. A child-local lookup never substitutes for the root-global fresh lookup.
3. Update a row when actor identity, declared scope, Helper write scope, index, pending intent, or published report owner changes. Route updates to current and previously matched peers to clear superseded facts. Preserve publication and delayed-report relay rules.
4. When declared write scopes may intersect, the published Job owner routes each matching row’s exact actor identity, responsible Producer identity, and the scope intersection or actual Git index identity that caused the match to every affected responsible Producer. Only the responsible Producers compare intended and actual changes and decide whether to split or combine contributions that overlap or depend on one another. Helpers report intended and actual changes through their parents to their responsible Producers; those Producers integrate and check Helper work. Helpers do not contact peers or negotiate. Producers resolve implementation boundaries without waiting for user direction.
5. Before its first staging, other index mutation, or commit for a contribution, every authorized index actor publishes a live pending intent through the route above and requests a fresh same-index lookup. The Supervisor processes intent installation and lookup of all live intents on that actual Git index as one serialized information action, completing it before processing the next install-and-lookup for that index. It returns every matching actor row, including responsible Producer identity, through the same routes. A queued publication, cached roster, or earlier lookup is not a completed lookup.

   After the completed lookup, only responsible Producers for returned matching rows contact one another directly and agree index-mutation and commit order for their contributions before any associated Worker proceeds. If a Helper or other non-Producer has the pending intent, its responsible Producer participates on its behalf; the actor reports through its parent and follows the Producer agreement without contacting peers or negotiating. With three or more Producer contenders, their agreements establish a consistent order. Only one actor mutates the shared index at a time. Keep each intent live through staging, the complete staged-result check, and commit, or until safely withdrawn or handed off by direct Producer agreement. A later Producer contacts only Producers returned as matching by its same-index lookup and agrees with them before any index mutation. A new intent or changed actual index requires a new lookup; an earlier agreement covers only the intent discussed.

   If Producer A’s lookup is processed before Producer B’s, A’s result cannot include B’s later intent. B’s lookup returns A while A’s intent is live, even if neither response has been delivered. B contacts A before staging; the Producers agree the order and any safe handoff of an in-progress operation. A follows its completed lookup and any resulting agreement. Lookup processing order grants no commit priority. The Supervisor supplies identity, scope, and index facts; the involved Producers choose the order. Actual Git index identity determines contention; branch identity supplies context.
6. Only responsible Producers of matching rows contact one another directly by exact runtime identity, by `write agent://<id>` (delivered at a message boundary while running; starts a turn when idle). Helpers and other non-Producer Workers report through their parent and follow their responsible Producer’s agreement; they do not contact peers or negotiate. The Supervisor and Orchestrators relay matching identity, scope, and index facts only, the Supervisor to each affected Job owner (for a child Job, through its parent owner); they do not assign content or schedule Git turns.

## Producer handoff commits and joint checkpoints

- When contributions overlap or depend on one another, the involved Producers compare the actual changes and agree whether to split or combine them. There is no automatic Job or lane priority across outcomes. Within an outcome, existing main-path-over-Fast precedence applies to overlap.
- Producers agree content boundaries and staging/commit order directly. A named Implementer and Fast Owner are Producers for their bounded contributions. Each commits its checked scope during handoff and reports the commit ID, immutable parent-to-commit range, scope, exclusions, focused checks, and limitations.
- Immediately before commit, the named Producer compares the complete staged diff and resulting index tree with the intended parent and exact checked, agreed contribution scope, including every joint contribution and exclusion. Commit only that result. If pre-existing staged content falls outside the agreed scope or cannot be isolated, preserve other Producers’ staged and unstaged work and coordinate the boundary and order directly with the exact affected Producers. Reinspect the complete staged result after that coordination; never silently include or clear another Producer’s work.
- After handoff, the Supervisor independently verifies the exact immutable range before dependent review or work. A Producer commit is a provisional checkpoint, not acceptance. Make repairs in a separate commit; do not amend or erase the earlier checkpoint.
- If Producer contributions cannot be separated cleanly, every contributing Producer agrees the exact bounded joint scope and selects one involved Producer to commit it. Each supplies and authorizes its own contribution. If the joint scope includes pre-existing authorized Supervisor-authored content, the Supervisor explicitly hands off and approves only that exact content for inclusion; the involved Producers still choose the boundary and committing Producer. Helpers supply their work and evidence through their responsible Producer and never commit. Include only completed, checked contributions; exclude unrelated and in-flight work.
- The joint handoff names every contributing Producer and separately identifies any handed-off pre-existing Supervisor-authored content with its original author, exact scope, and provenance. Map each contribution to its agreed changes, Job/outcome where applicable, focused-check evidence, limitations, and exclusions. List every affected outcome and the single immutable parent-to-commit range. The named Producer commits only that scope after the complete staged-result check. The Supervisor independently verifies the entire range and sends it with the contribution mapping to every affected Job owner (for a child Job, through its parent owner).
- Every affected outcome retains its normal review and acceptance requirements. Review interacting contributions together and attach integrated evidence to every affected outcome. Its Job owner accounts for criteria and evidence; the Supervisor accepts. No contributing Producer supplies the required independent review of its own contribution. A joint checkpoint does not merge outcomes or count as acceptance.
- A joint checkpoint is the narrow exception to Fast’s separate checkpoint per changed issue when contributions are inseparable. Preserve evidence for each issue; other Fast issues keep separate checkpoints. Repairs remain separate commits.

## Worker and Helper boundaries

- Any Worker, including a Helper, may recursively delegate bounded support within its inherited assignment, authorization, read/write limits, and the Supervisor’s global capacity decisions. Delegation never enlarges authority.
- Every Helper reports through its spawning Worker. Each parent accounts for descendants’ scope, writes, evidence, results, and shutdown. The Job owner accounts for delegated Writers before outcome closure. Helpers add no Job or outcome and do not create a lane by themselves.
- A reviewer personally establishes its independent baseline, verifies evidence, supplies its required verdict, and performs its assigned E2E. Helpers at any depth cannot replace those duties or carry conclusions across independent reviews. Reviewers may delegate bounded support; the current Job owner dispatches required workflow reviews and E2E assignments for each outcome.
- Implementation authority must exist through the full delegation chain. Helpers return changes through their parents to the named Implementer or Fast Owner. That Producer integrates, checks, and commits the bounded contribution under the rules above.
