# ECI Job Orchestrator

This module defines Job-level coordination within one ECI lifecycle. It changes logical ownership only; it does not change Worker runtime authority, hooks, or planner authority.

## Counts and ownership

- **N** counts admitted outcomes until acceptance or completed cancellation closure, including queued, waiting, stuck, post-Fast, and cancellation-cleanup outcomes. Reopening an accepted outcome counts it again. N governs lifecycle and acceptance only; it never publishes or retires Orchestrators.
- Give each independently requested root work package one stable **Job** identity. A Job may recursively spawn child Jobs under the admission rule below. Record one immutable parent Job for each child; root Jobs have no parent. Ordinary outcome decomposition, extensions, repairs, iterations, main/Fast paths, Helpers, owner transfers, and shared targets retain their outcome’s Job unless admitted as a distinct child package. Each outcome has one home Job; reopening restores it and its ancestry.
- **J** counts every unclosed Job once across the whole lifecycle. A Job closes only when all home outcomes are accepted or cancellation-closed, all child Jobs are closed, all subtree Writers are observed stopped, and all produced reports are accounted for. Pending transfers, report drains, and cancellation cleanup keep the Job counted. A parent remains counted while descendants continue, even after its home outcomes close.
- Each active Job has exactly one published Orchestrator, including a single Job. That Orchestrator coordinates all outcomes in its Job directly through their Workers; do not add a separate outcome owner beneath it. Publication rules: [Publication, transfer, and report drain](#publication-transfer-and-report-drain).
- A new outcome within an active Job changes that Job owner’s outcome set, not the number of Orchestrators. N transitions alone never create or retire an Orchestrator.

| Active work | Logical ownership |
| --- | --- |
| Job A contains A1/A2; J = 1, N = 2 | Orchestrator A coordinates both outcomes. |
| Job A contains A1/A2; Job B contains B1; J = 2, N = 3 | Exactly two Orchestrators: A coordinates A1/A2; B coordinates B1. |
| Job B satisfies the closure predicate, leaving A1/A2; J = 1 while N = 2 | Orchestrator A continues to coordinate A1/A2. |
| A → B → C; N = 0; C has pending Writer/report cleanup; J = 3 | A/B/C remain counted until the subtree satisfies closure; then J = 0. |

At N = 0, the Supervisor performs ordinary lifecycle teardown only after every Job satisfies the closure predicate above.

## Recursive child admission and closure

- The current Job owner may admit a bounded child package within inherited user authorization: decomposition, delegated deliverables, or related work covered by the task mandate. Producers and Helpers propose a child to their Job owner; spawning a Helper alone creates no Job. Record the parent, requested outcome, authorization source, scope, dependencies, acceptance criteria, and reporting route. Children may admit further children by the same rule. A child Job alone creates no authorization gap.
- Admit new work only within that authority. Relevance, a discovery, severity, or a deadline alone does not authorize unrelated work. Keep unadmitted separate-outcome observations as follow-ups; send only an actual authorization gap to the Supervisor (for a child Job, through its parent owner). A bounded, authorized repair that no required work awaits may be a child; it does not become a parent acceptance criterion merely through admission.
- When no worker advances work that a required outcome's acceptance or proof depends on, assign it at once in the current Job or as an admitted child Job instead of parking that outcome. Keep a worker advancing it until it lands, except while it waits, with proof under the Stuck work rule in `~/.omp/agent/RULES.md`, for capacity or for the user's answer to a question the Supervisor put under [user-owned decisions](../../../eci-binding-contract.md#decisions).
- Report admission and the effective owner route upward through parent owners to the root Supervisor. Apply the publisher protocol below within root capacity decisions. The root Supervisor maintains global N/J and ownership visibility across all depths. Parent edges survive transfers and J changes; ownership transfer never flattens the Job tree. Protected control and acceptance remain with the root Supervisor.
- Each child has its own requirements and full ECI pipeline: concurrent main and Fast Producers, fresh independent critics, post-Fast restart, required verification, and separate acceptance evidence. Parent exploration, Fast success, reviews, or checkpoint never substitute for the child's pipeline. Dispatch ready independent parent, child, and sibling work concurrently; serialize only actual dependencies, overlapping mutable state, or capacity.
- Child owners report progress, findings, reported ranges, review evidence, and closure recommendations upward to their parent owner, which relays material state to the Supervisor. Supervisor-owned decisions from a child Job take the same parent route. The Supervisor sends its decisions and other follow-ups for a child Job, including those for its Producers and Helpers, through the parent owner, not directly; protected controls such as `write proc://<id>/kill` stay direct. Parent ownership accounts for descendants but does not confer Producer write authority or independent-review authority.
- Accept each outcome independently under Supervisor authority. A parent's home outcomes may finish while descendants continue; apply the canonical Job closure predicate above. Cancel a Job recursively through its descendants, preserve changes and evidence, and leave unrelated siblings running. Child completion or cancellation never tears down the root lifecycle. Only the root Supervisor performs root teardown after the whole tree closes.

## Authority and reporting

- The Supervisor owns root outcome admission, global admission visibility and closure, N/J tracking, global capacity, protected controls, cross-Job visibility, acceptance, and lifecycle teardown. Current Job owners admit authorized child packages under the recursive admission rule.
- An Orchestrator owns routine dispatch, continuation, sequencing, dependencies, evidence reconciliation, progress, [stall decisions](coordinator.md#stall-decisions), child admission and child-owner publication, and closure preparation for its published Job. It coordinates every home outcome directly and its child Jobs through their owners, and loads the task-local review policy for review dispatch. It cannot enlarge authorization, publish an unrelated owner, accept work, or close the root lifecycle.
- An Orchestrator, including a parent owner for its child Jobs, completes its full Job through acceptance-ready gate evidence. It dispatches each ECI-assigned step without awaiting Supervisor or publisher confirmation. A message that names one step or asks for a report neither narrows the Job nor permits holding the next step; send the report and continue. Stop only under [stall decisions](coordinator.md#stall-decisions), Stuck work in `~/.omp/agent/RULES.md`, or a Supervisor-owned decision that blocks the next step.
- Preserve each outcome’s requirements, bounded assignments, Producer identities, Fast path, post-Fast sequence, and acceptance evidence. The Job's outcomes share its main-path iterations, independent reviews, and required E2E. In each iteration that runs Step 2, dispatch it over all the Job's remaining work; dispatch each iteration's Step 3 as Producer assignments that together implement all of it; dispatch its Step 4 once every assignment is handed off, never per contribution, commit, or component. See the [iteration rule](../SKILL.md#iteration-rule). One Job owner never combines the requirements or acceptance of its outcomes.
- Name each assignment’s Job, parent chain, outcome, Supervisor runtime identity, and current published Job-owner runtime identity. Use [canonical assignment preparation](../../context-ledger/requirement-assignment.md) with that context in operational instructions and forward its returned payload unchanged through native tools. Workers on an established direct-message report route send task-local reports to the current owner's `agent://<id>` using the Assignment reports transport of the Parking rule in `~/.omp/agent/RULES.md`. Use [violation re-read escalation](../SKILL.md#violation-re-read-escalation) for its exception to ordinary report and relay routes. During transfer, follow [report routing and recovery](#publication-transfer-and-report-drain), including after failed delivery. Child owners report through parent owners; send Supervisor-owned decisions to the Supervisor (for a child Job, through its parent owner).
- A Producer sends its committed handoff through the Job owner to the Supervisor, which relays the reported ranges to every other affected Job owner (for a child Job, through its parent owner).
- Job coordination does not grant authority to assign disputed content or schedule commits, except as a [stall decision](coordinator.md#stall-decisions) allows. The Supervisor and Orchestrators report identity and scope facts, plus any mistake notice the [role rule](../SKILL.md#activation-and-invariants) requires. Producers resolve conflicts directly under [Commits](#commits); Helpers report through their parents and follow their responsible Producer’s agreement.
- **Messages to a Job:** the Supervisor and Orchestrators apply the [role rule](../SKILL.md#activation-and-invariants) to everything they send to an owner or Worker they coordinate, including every `task` field, brief, relay, reply, follow-up, and correction, and to their dispatch reports.
  - Cite each relevant user message under the Sourced constraints rule in `~/.omp/agent/RULES.md` for the `exact user source → faithful requested outcome → bounded scope` chain. Cite, with its author, only the part of an earlier passage the user's words refer to. Content the user chooses from it, such as "do option 2", is user-set; approving an analysis, such as "good tensions", sets nothing beyond the user's words.
  - Attribute your own observations, views, and mistake notices as the role rule sets; cite findings with their authors under the Sourced constraints rule. Tag your readings of the user's words `[INFERENCE]`.
  - Keep the required operational instructions, including the SSOT checkout and branch, any non-SSOT development checkout, and the [commit rule](#commits). Scope gives authorization bounds and excludes what the user excluded, outcomes the user did not request, other owners' scope, and unrelated dirty work. Acceptance restates the whole requested outcome, treating user examples under [User requirements](../../../eci-binding-contract.md#user-requirements). Targets, further exclusions, and further acceptance criteria come only from the role rule's design sources. Route skills from the request, user-set constraints, evidence, and any Step 2 output; the Job owner routes design-dependent skills. Policy files the recipient loads apply without restating.
  - Report a dispatch as an assignment under [Work estimates](../../../eci-binding-contract.md#work-estimates), with the requested outcome as its expected result. Report designs and changes only as the Job produces them.

## Publication, transfer, and report drain

- The publisher is the root Supervisor for root Jobs and the current parent Job owner for child Jobs. For initial publication, the publisher is the named interim owner; during transfer, the outgoing owner remains effective until publication. Child publication follows root capacity decisions; a changed parent owner takes over pending child transfers and report holds.
- **Initial admission:** before the first Worker spawn for any Job, root or child, the Job is declared in the ledger as `work:<job>:job` (`owner`, `title`, `state: active`, and an `outcomes` link to each admitted outcome record) and its Orchestrator is published. This ordering is the one exception to [ledger update priority](../../context-ledger/SKILL.md#update-points); it is a spawn order, not a permission check. Do not spawn a Worker for a Job until its Orchestrator is published. For multiple Jobs admitted together, prepare one Orchestrator candidate per Job (the prepared, unpublished owner) from the complete admitted set; publish one owner per Job, not one per outcome. If capacity is short, the publisher queues the candidate and publishes it when capacity frees: the Supervisor for a root Job, the parent Job owner for a child Job.
- **Job publication:** each publisher prepares one Orchestrator for each active Job it publishes without one, under the spawn rule in the Initial admission bullet above. Each transfer covers every home outcome.
- **Job admission:** prepare and publish its Orchestrator. Adding or closing outcomes in existing Jobs changes their owners’ assignments, not the number of Orchestrators.
- **Before publication:** the outgoing owner stops new dispatch only for the transferring Job, finishes or accounts for its active coordination call, and transfers requirements, scopes, assignments, evidence, outstanding reports, and current state for every home outcome, plus child-owner routes and subtree accountability. Hold processing, not delivery. Before a hold, the publisher gives Workers and immediate child owners a reachable receiver that preserves their reports for the effective owner. Tell each sender which Job’s transfer designated the receiver. Route Supervisor-owned decisions to the Supervisor (for a child Job, through its parent owner). Existing Workers continue bounded work. If the outgoing owner is unreachable (its `write agent://<id>` failed), the publisher performs this transfer from the outgoing owner's `history://<id>` transcript and the ledger.
- **Publication:** Publish only after the candidate accepts the current transfer proposal. Preparation, acceptance of a different proposal, and report custody do not count. If acceptance is missing, send the proposal by ordinary `write agent://<id>` and use the Parking rule to await the answer or restore its source. If the required path fails, restore it or withdraw as below. Before switching, set the report destination and establish a usable `wait` source for the next inherited report the candidate still needs to receive. No separate source is needed for a report the publisher holds and can deliver with the publication decision. Use direct delivery if the candidate can await the authorized source. Otherwise the candidate requests the actual report from the publisher by ordinary `write agent://<id>` in its current run. The publisher requests it from the Worker or immediate child owner that owes it, directly or through a reachable outgoing owner that can await it. Verify every relay leg under the Parking rule; an address, notice, or acknowledgement is not a usable source. Check the waits the parties will actually enter, including runtime-owned tasks and the spawning parent. Do not publish on a route that would leave a party unable to wait for the report. If the publisher’s owned candidate is waiting only for the publisher and that blocks the publisher’s report wait, use a usable independent route or withdraw the unpublished preparation. On withdrawal, send the candidate an ordinary terminal release and retain the report request if recovery still needs it. Once the cycle clears, wait for the report and recompute with it in hand.

  Recheck Job membership, scope, effective owner, and current parent owner. Do not publish if the required path or publication notice cannot be delivered; restore it or withdraw. The outgoing owner remains effective until the switch. If unreachable, the publisher recovers available reports, restores a usable source, or arranges authorized continuation without dispatching the Job. Preparation grants no dispatch authority.

  Publish one effective owner. Notify the incoming and outgoing owners and Workers, and give immediate child owners their new upward route. Arrange the notice route before the switch; announce the decision only after the switch. Use an ordinary `write agent://<id>` from an authorized sender whose write does not settle a still-needed reply. On a publisher relay, use the report source for a separate notice when that source can send it: it relays the publisher’s decision while the publisher’s report reply remains owed. If no such sender is available, or receipt of its notice is not established when the actual report is ready, include the publication decision with the actual report. Do not hold a ready report for a notice acknowledgement. A withdrawal uses a terminal release, not a publication notice.

  On publication, end the hold. The current publisher accounts for every unprocessed held report until it reaches the effective owner. Collect reports from the designated receiver or arrange delivery by that holder under **Report routing and recovery**. If a holder is unreachable, recover its available reports or restore the route; do not ask a finished reporter to resend. Keep the designated route for reports already in flight until delivery or until a usable route takes over.

  The publisher keeps a usable source for its own next needed result. If it still needs the incoming owner's handoff and has no usable source for it, request the specific handoff on the established upward route. Include the request in the report reply, or in a notice that does not settle a different needed reply.

  If scope or the parent owner changes, recompute or withdraw. On withdrawal, end the hold and return preserved reports to the effective owner, or to the current publisher for recovery if that owner is unreachable. Route missing notices through the current publisher.
- **Report routing and recovery:** keep each needed report path usable until delivery or a usable replacement takes over. A relay owes its requester the actual report or, if it will not fulfill the request, an ordinary terminal reply naming the request and releasing it. Handing over report custody does not settle a reply: the old responder delivers it or releases its requester when a replacement takes over. On withdrawal, release requests made only for that transfer; a release is not a report and grants no dispatch authority. An ordinary write back can settle the runtime reply for the pair, not just the request named in its body. Include other owed results that are ready, or use an authorized route that preserves their sources. If a still-needed source is lost, the requester restores it under the Parking rule before waiting again.

  After a failed report write, preserve the undelivered report. If the receiver was designated by a transfer, send the report to the current publisher of the Job whose transfer designated that receiver, even after cutover. Otherwise send it to the current publisher of the Job whose owner should process the report. If that publisher is unavailable, use that Job’s existing upward ownership route for recovery. The recovery publisher restores delivery of the actual report to the effective owner and settles or replaces the affected relay requests without taking dispatch. After publication, repair the route without undoing the ownership switch. Continue authorized recovery while required work remains; failed delivery alone does not justify suspension.
- **Violation escalation routing:** The owner of the first published root Job enforces all [Supervisor violations](../SKILL.md#violation-re-read-escalation). Include that Job's current escalation address in root routing notices. Before first publication, preserve findings for that owner.

  For escalation only, a retired Job uses its last published owner. If the retired address fails, use the existing upward recovery route: the nearest reachable published ancestor takes over, or, if none, the Supervisor designates a reachable root Job owner. Announce the successor's address on the ordinary route and retain the original Job anchor. For the first published root Job, that escalation successor also handles future Supervisor findings. If none is reachable, preserve the report for the authorized successor. This neither reopens the Job nor grants dispatch.

  Use ordinary transfer and report recovery for ladder state, including for a retired owner's successor. Carry each occurrence, earlier requests and lists, any pending step and its request reference, and finder return routes. The successor reconciles requests already sent and redirects a pending list without demanding another read.
- **Retirement and delayed reports:** an owner retires from dispatch when its entire Job subtree closes. Closing its last home outcome does not retire an owner with unfinished descendants. Stay published for relay until already-produced reports are accounted for. Former, withdrawn, and non-current owners relay reports through the current parent route to the Supervisor without dispatch. A former owner forwards reports already in hand as ready work. If explicitly assigned to relay a future report, it applies the Parking rule until it forwards the report or a usable route takes over. Relay neither restores dispatch authority nor keeps the former owner published. The Supervisor reopens a closed outcome when a report states a defect or unmet criterion against it, leaving its finding decisions to [Step 4](../SKILL.md#step-handoffs), and forwards an unprocessed active-outcome report to its current owner (for a child Job, through its parent owner). Do not duplicate already-routed reports or issued reviews.
- Ownership transfer preserves Producer and reviewer identities, assignments, and runtime ancestry. It does not restart Workers, wait for their acknowledgements, or stop Writers solely because ownership changed. Root teardown uses the canonical closure predicate.

Workers on a direct-message report route explicitly deliver progress reports to the destination in [Authority and reporting](#authority-and-reporting); final-report and unresolved-dependency timing and transport follow Assignment reports in the Parking rule in `~/.omp/agent/RULES.md`. Preserve the Job, outcome, assignment, original source, report, and evidence references in every Worker report and relay. A published owner processes reports within its assignment except during a transfer hold. A non-current owner relays through the current logical parent route without dispatch, implementation, review, disposition, acceptance, or lifecycle action; root-Job reports go to the Supervisor. Forward only reports that still need current-owner processing; already-routed evidence needs no duplicate followup. A timeout alone does not trigger a drain.

## Job hold and tool errors

- The published Orchestrator holds its Job until the Job closes under the closure predicate or the Supervisor tears it down. A report, an outcome ready for acceptance, or a stand-by does not release the Job. See [Counts and ownership](#counts-and-ownership).
- When nothing is actionable, apply the Parking rule in `~/.omp/agent/RULES.md`. On a tool error, read `proc://` to check the Workers, then continue actionable work or follow Parking. A tool error never releases the Job.
- A cancel of this owner's own run, by the Supervisor or parent owner with `write proc://<id>/kill`, or by a user or a runtime stop, is not a tool error: it ends the turn, and `Operation aborted` from a tool is the cancel signature. If the turn continues after that result, treat it as a tool error under the bullet above. The Job stays open and this owner stays published. On revival after a cancel, it reads `proc://` for Workers that may still run, then acts on ready work, reports changed state, and applies the Parking rule when idle. Cancelling one of its Workers returns a normal result and does not end the turn. See [Counts and ownership](#counts-and-ownership).
- At each idle checkpoint (the Parking rule in `~/.omp/agent/RULES.md`), it sends its parent owner (for a root Job, the Supervisor) only Job state not yet reported, at `agent://<parent owner>` under that rule's Assignment reports transport. It stays the published owner while waiting or parked. See [Authority and reporting](#authority-and-reporting).

## Active Job compliance

The current Job owner immediately critiques significant-movement and final Worker reports against actual work, original requirements, and applicable ECI duties. Check role authority, applicable ECI dependency skills and their methods, model selection, delegation, and completion evidence; a positive report or unchanged assignment is insufficient. Enforce concrete ECI-duty deviations through [violation re-read escalation](../SKILL.md#violation-re-read-escalation). Carry [test-efficiency citations](../SKILL.md#test-efficiency) into the existing Step 4 packet. Return missing or ambiguous proof evidence to its owner; continue unaffected work.

Retain the last roster-review time in existing coordination state. About every half-hour while the Job actively executes, refresh its active roster at the next normal coordination cycle (a revival counts): actor, runtime parent, assigned role/profile, and actual current work, including Helpers. The current owner accounts for descendants. Explicitly confirm or critique each actor's ECI compliance from current assignments, reports, and relevant evidence; retain the verdict, correction, or unknown and next check. Reuse current reports; request a focused activity update when stale or unclear. Send the roster and findings to the Supervisor in the ordinary Job update. Update changed entries when significant movement is processed and carry the last check through ownership transfer. Do one current review when due; add no catch-up bursts, idle wakeups, background polling, separate registry format, or checker agents.

At each roster review and idle checkpoint, check every Fast Producer of an unaccepted outcome. When the Parking rule calls for reviving a stopped Fast Producer, write its remaining outcome and the outcome's current full scope to its `agent://<id>` at once. Do not revive a Producer already using `wait`, idle after [genuine Fast completion](fast-path.md#outcome-continuation-and-completion), or legitimately suspended under the Parking rule. A pending Step 2, review, main-path change, or disposition alone neither suspends Fast work nor permits parking.

Investigate each leaf execution that runs far over its estimate. Count an execution's elapsed time from its start clock read, or else its assignment time, and pause the count while your Job is paused by the user. At each roster review and each processed report, compare the elapsed time of each leaf execution in your Job's work trees, while it runs and when a processed leaf-completion report closes it, with the upper bound of its planned range for one execution. When the elapsed time first reaches twice that bound, such as 1 hour against a planned 15–30 minutes, find the cause at that cycle from the executing actors' current reports, `proc://` state and `history://` transcripts. Causes include an idle or stuck Worker, a restart or lost work, work wider than the leaf's scope, a stuck dependency, a repeated review or gate loop, and a leaf sized smaller than its work. Mitigate within your authority: revive or redirect an idle or stuck Worker, cancelling a hung run first, return an assignment to its leaf's scope, resolve or reorder around the dependency, and apply a [stall decision](coordinator.md#stall-decisions) where one applies. Send the cause, any mitigation and its result to the Supervisor in the ordinary Job update. Check that result again at each later roster review while the execution runs.

## Commits

- Only Producers commit; a named Implementer and Fast Owner are Producers for their bounded contributions. Assign every new tracked repository contribution—including documentation, configuration, analysis, and administrative deliverables—to a Producer. Supervisors and Orchestrators author no new tracked contribution, stage nothing, and commit nothing. Helpers return work through their responsible Producer and never commit.
- Producers commit their own checked work whenever they choose: only their own and their Helpers' lines, or an agreed joint commit below, under the [Git rules](../../../eci-binding-contract.md#git).
- On a conflict with another Producer, contact that Producer directly and resolve it. Only after trying and failing to resolve a repeated conflict, escalate it to your Job owner for a [stall decision](coordinator.md#stall-decisions). Within an outcome, main-path-over-Fast precedence applies to overlap.
- The handoff reports commit IDs with their parent-to-commit ranges, scope, exclusions, checks, and limitations. The Supervisor trusts that report without re-verifying the ranges. A commit is a provisional checkpoint, not acceptance.
- When contributions are inseparable, the contributing Producers agree the joint scope and one of them commits it, naming every contributor in the handoff. Every affected outcome keeps its own review and acceptance; no contributor reviews its own contribution.
- The [typed Worker Git route](../../../eci/hooks/gate-escape-hatches.md) (`eci-worker-git`) is built but not launcher-wired, so it and its `run-once` exception are unavailable during active ECI.

## Worker and Helper boundaries

- Any Worker, including a Helper, may recursively delegate bounded support within its inherited assignment, authorization, read/write limits, and the Supervisor’s global capacity decisions. Delegation never enlarges authority.
- Every Helper reports through its spawning Worker. Each parent accounts for descendants’ scope, writes, evidence, results, and shutdown. The Job owner accounts for delegated Writers before outcome closure. Helpers add no Job or outcome and do not create a lane by themselves.
- A reviewer personally establishes its independent baseline, verifies evidence, supplies its required verdict, and performs its assigned E2E. Helpers at any depth cannot replace those duties or carry conclusions across independent reviews. Reviewers may delegate bounded support; the current Job owner dispatches the Job's required workflow reviews and E2E assignments.
- Implementation authority must exist through the full delegation chain. Helpers return changes through their parents to the named Implementer or Fast Owner. That Producer integrates, checks, and commits the bounded contribution under the rules above.
